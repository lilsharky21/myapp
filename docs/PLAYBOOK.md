# The Thesis Journal playbook: how to pick stocks with this app

The app can't tell you what will go up. Nobody can. What it can do is make you
slow down, look at the same evidence a professional would, write down *why*, and
learn from every call. This routine is built around that.

## The funnel

```
Discover / search  →  Watchlist  →  Full research  →  Decide & size  →  Own  →  Review  →  Close & learn
   (find)              (shortlist)    (check)          (plan)           (hold)   (every 90 days)  (Your record)
```

## 1. Find ideas (weekly, ~15 minutes)

- Open **Discover**. It scans every NYSE and Nasdaq company's annual report and keeps
  the strongest ~120, so you'll see lesser-known companies next to famous ones. Read the
  green "why it's here" line, and tap **+** on anything worth a closer look. Try
  **Size → Mid** or **Small** to find companies most people haven't heard of yet.
  Pick the time frame you're investing for:
  - **Best overall** first: good businesses whose price is also rising.
  - **Long term** (years): *Quality at a fair price* and *Compounders* are the most
    beginner-friendly.
  - **Months**: *Growth speeding up* and *Growth at a reasonable price* find companies
    whose profits are improving.
  - **Swing** (days to weeks): *Pullback in an uptrend* is the classic. Always tap
    **Check the charts** and only consider the ✓ ones, keep them small, and set a stop.
- Also add companies you already understand (things you use or see everywhere).
  Knowing the business is an edge.
- Open anything interesting. It costs nothing to look. Tap **Add** only if you can
  already say one sentence about why it might be a good business.
- Keep the watchlist to about **10–15 stocks**. More than that and you stop
  following them properly.

## 2. Research one stock properly (~20 minutes each)

Go through the tabs in this order:

1. **Overview**: the three ratings. When Wall Street, the App score and the AI
   all agree, that's a stronger signal. When they disagree, find out why.
2. **Research**: the AI's note. On your phone, tap **Ask my Mac**. Read the bottom
   line, the bear/base/bull values, and **What would change the view**.
3. **Financials**: is revenue growing year after year? Is there real free cash flow?
4. **Valuation**: move the DCF sliders. Look at **what today's price assumes**: if
   the price needs 25% growth a year for 10 years, that's a high bar.
5. **Technicals**: not a reason to buy, just timing. Buying while the price is below
   its 200-day average means you're going against the trend.
6. **Earnings / Investors / News**: surprises, what insiders did, and anything that
   could break the story.

Then write your **thesis** (Edit): one sentence you can check later, plus a bull and
a bear case. Example: *"Revenue keeps growing 15%+ a year and margins stay above 60%."*

## 3. Before you buy

- Tick the **before-you-buy checklist** in the Research tab.
- Pick your **stop**: the price where you'd admit you're wrong. The Research tab's
  "Prices to watch" gives natural levels (60-day low, 52-week low, 200-day average).
- Use **How much should I buy?** Risking about 1% of your money per idea means one
  bad call can't hurt you much.
- Set **price alerts** at your stop and your target (Journal tab → Price alerts).
- Check **Next earnings** in Overview. Many people wait until after the report,
  because prices can jump 10% either way that day.
- Compare with alternatives: **Compare** view. If this isn't one of your top 3–5
  ideas, why buy it instead of those?

## 4. When you buy

- **Log the trade** in the Journal tab (shares and price). The app tracks your cost and P/L.
- Add a **dated note**: why now? What are you expecting in the next 3–6 months?

## 5. While you own it (a few minutes a week)

- **Today** tells you what needs you: earnings coming up, alerts that went off,
  big moves, reviews due.
- After each earnings report, add a note: did it support the thesis or not?
- Every 90 days the app asks for a **review**. Re-read your thesis. If it's still
  true, tap *Still holds*. If it's changed, **edit the thesis**. The app keeps the
  old version so you can see how your thinking changed.
- The price going down is not the same as the thesis being wrong. A broken thesis
  is a reason to sell; a scary headline usually isn't.

## 6. Close and learn

- When you sell (or stop watching), **Close the idea**: pick an honest verdict
  (Called it / Too early / Lucky / Wrong) and write one lesson.
- Check **Your record** (Closed tab) every month or two:
  - Are you beating the S&P 500? If not, a simple index fund might be the better choice
    for most of your money. That's normal: most professionals don't beat it either.
  - Do your high-conviction ideas actually do better? If not, be more humble with sizing.
  - Read your lessons. Patterns show up fast.

## Rules of thumb for beginners

- Start small. Your first year is for learning the process, not for making money.
- Never put money you'll need soon into single stocks.
- Diversify: no single stock should be a huge share of your money.
- An AI note is a research assistant, not an oracle. It can misread numbers. Check
  anything important yourself, especially the bear case.
- In the US, people under 18 can only own stocks through a **custodial account**
  that a parent or guardian opens and controls. Talk it through with them.

*This app is for research and learning. It isn't financial advice.*
